## About

During the first 9-months of our apprenticeship we received our preliminary training from Kingston College. Here, while already working on the robot in the background, six friends and I decided to also enter the F1 in Schools competition. The competition has teams of students from around the world design, build, market and race small CO2 canister powered cars approximately resembling those of actual F1 teams. Our entry, Tiro (Latin for apprentice), was the first all-apprentice team. I was responsible for much of the car’s design along with the graphics. The competition consists of three stages; regionals, nationals and the international final. Progression through the stages requires teams to consistently out-perform most competitors both technically (how fast the car is) and operationally (how well managed the team and work are).
Our lack-lustre QA in the regionals (and the resultant “critical” rule violation in the car as presented) cost us 1st place. A mistake from which we learnt and in doing so successfully achieved “English National Champions” in the second stage. The final, held over the course of a week in Kuala Lumpur, involved 55 teams of which we came 7th, an achievement of which I am very proud. We were also recognised with the “Research and Development Award” sponsored by McLaren for our extensive R&D efforts.
In addition to our work on the car and its documentation, participation in the final required us to produce a glossy “Pit Display” (similar to trade show booth) and fundraise over £30,000 to cover our working costs and the team’s expenses. My experience with Tiro was as gruelling as it was enlightening. It was another excellent early introduction to working with a team of engineers under pressure, doing difficult logistics, bickering about fluid dynamics, producing legible documentation and acquiring funding.
